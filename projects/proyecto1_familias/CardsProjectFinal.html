Single mothers face steep income gaps as poverty persists across U.S. states

For many single mothers, escaping poverty requires a bigger income gap than most states can close. This project uses data from the U.S. Census Bureau’s American Community Survey 5-year estimates to compare how these households are affected across states, focusing on poverty levels, the number of families impacted, racial and ethnic concentration and the income gap that separates many of these families from economic stability.

Each card shows four key indicators for a state: how many single-mother families are living in poverty, the share they represent, the racial or ethnic group that makes up the largest portion of this population and the amount of income still needed to move above the poverty threshold. Together, these measures show that poverty is not distributed evenly. In some places, the number of affected families is especially large. In others, the financial shortfall is steeper, suggesting that escaping poverty may require a much bigger income jump.

Use the filters to sort the cards by states with the largest affected populations, by places where the income deficit is highest or by the racial majority among single mothers living in poverty. The label “High need for money” marks states where the income deficit rises above the median shortfall across the dataset, helping highlight where the burden is especially severe.


Reporting and data analysis by Nicol León Arge. Developed as part of the Coding the News class at the Craig Newmark Graduate School of Journalism at CUNY, this project draws on U.S. Census Bureau data from the American Community Survey (ACS) 5-year estimates to examine the economic conditions of households led by single mothers across the United States.
